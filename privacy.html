Oakhall, Privacy Policy

Last updated 22 September 2026

Oakhall has no account system, no analytics, no payments, and no server run by its developer. Almost everything it knows about you stays on your phone. The exceptions are listed below, in full.

Oakhall is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by the University of Toronto. It is an independent app that signs in to U of T's own services on your behalf, using your own credentials, at your request.

Your UTORid password

The app never sees it. Signing in happens inside a web view showing U of T's own login page, served from U of T's servers over an encrypted connection. The app draws a frame around that page; it does not read the fields, and it has no code that could. Nothing types your password, stores it, or transmits it anywhere.

What the app keeps afterwards is the session, the same cookies a browser keeps after you log in. They live on your phone and are sent only back to utoronto.ca.

Services the app contacts

All of these are contacted directly by your phone. None of them report back to the developer.

  • U of T, acorn.utoronto.ca, q.utoronto.ca, and the login provider idpz.utorauth.utoronto.ca, to read your own timetable and coursework.
  • Duo Security and Instructure (sso.canvaslms.com), part of U of T's own sign-in; they see what they would see if you signed in with a browser.
  • U of T Timetable Builder (api.easi.utoronto.ca), a public course catalogue. Requests carry a course code and no identity.
  • Concept3D (assets.concept3d.com), campus photos, loaded only when you open a place that has one.
  • Anthropic (api.anthropic.com), only if you add your own API key and use the Assistant. Your question and relevant context are sent under your own account.
  • Apple CloudKit, for Friends: your username, who you are connected to, and, only if you turn it on, your position while you are on campus.

Friends

Friends is built on Apple's CloudKit, under your own iCloud account. There is no server belonging to the developer anywhere in it. Two different things are stored in two different places, and the difference is the important part:

WhatWhereWho can read it
Your profile: username, name, photo, bio, and who you are connected toCloudKit's public databaseAnyone who knows your username, and the developer through Apple's CloudKit dashboard
Your timetableYour own private CloudKit databaseOnly the friends you share it with. Not the developer

Sharing uses Apple's own sharing mechanism: your schedule stays in your private database, and each friend is named on it individually by Apple. Someone who is not on the list cannot read it, that is enforced by Apple, not by this app being careful.

What is still visible to the developer: your chosen handle and your friend list. Not your schedule, not your classes, not where you are. The handle is not anonymous if you pick your own name for it, and none of it is end-to-end encrypted. It is not sold, analysed, or used for anything, but "the developer cannot see any of it" would be untrue, so it is not claimed.

Your profile is public. The name, photo and one-line bio you choose are visible to anyone who knows your username, and to the developer. That is what they are for: a handle with no face is a hard thing to trust before you share your week with it. Leave them blank and the app shows your initials instead. Your timetable and your location are not part of your profile and are never public.

Reporting. If someone's photo or bio is offensive, impersonates someone, or is spam, you can report it from their profile. Reports are not anonymous to the developer and are not shown to the person reported. Removing someone cuts off everything you share with them immediately.

Within Friends:

  • You choose a username. It is a handle you pick, not your UTORid, and not your legal name unless you make it one.
  • New friends see busy times only, when you are free, with no course names or rooms, unless you deliberately promote them to see your full timetable. The two versions are stored separately; a busy-only friend is not shown a filtered copy of the full one, they are given a different record that never had the course names in it.
  • Removing a friend, or reducing what they can see, takes them off that share. Their access ends there and then, at Apple's level.
  • If you never use Friends, none of this data is ever created.

Location

Location does two different things in this app, and only one of them ever leaves your phone.

On the map, and for walking times. Showing where you are on the campus map and working out the real walk to your next class both happen entirely on your device. Nothing about that is transmitted, to the developer or to anyone else. Refuse the permission and the rest of the app works normally.

Sharing your location with friends. This is off until you turn it on, and it is the only thing in this app that sends your position anywhere.

If you turn location sharing on:

  • Only the friends you pick. Nobody can see you until you select them by name, one at a time. Turning someone off takes effect immediately, and they are not told.
  • Only while you're on campus. Your phone works out whether you're on campus before anything is sent. Off campus, nothing is transmitted at all, and what was previously shared is deleted, not hidden. Your friends see "not on campus", not the spot where you left.
  • Your position, and the building you're in. Friends you have chosen see where you are on the campus map, labelled with the building you're in when you're in one. This is a precise position, it is meant for finding each other, and it is why it only works on campus, only for people you name, and only while you leave it switched on.
  • No history is kept. There is one current position per person, overwritten each time it updates and dropped after two hours. There is no trail of where you have been, not on your phone, not in the cloud, and not available to the developer, because it is never recorded in the first place.
  • As you move, roughly every 50 metres. Enough to tell one building from the next. This uses battery while it is switched on, which is part of why it is off by default and why turning it off stops the app using location in the background at all.
  • Turning it off is one switch, at the top of Friends → My location, and it stops sharing straight away.

Your position is stored in Apple's CloudKit while it is being shared. Friends you have selected can read it; it is deleted when you leave campus, pause sharing, or two hours pass.

This app is not a location tracker and is not designed to be used as one. It will not show where someone lives, where they work, or where they were yesterday, because it does not collect any of that.

Analytics and tracking

There are none. No analytics SDK, no crash reporter, no advertising identifier, no third-party trackers, and no profile of you anywhere. The app does not track you across apps or websites.

Age

Oakhall is intended for university students. It is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Location sharing is for students 18 and over. By turning it on you confirm you are 18 or older. This is a condition of use, not something the app checks. We do not ask your age and we do not ask for identification, and we would rather say so plainly than imply a verification that does not happen.

Everything else in the app, your timetable, assignments, the campus map, Eats, the waitlist watcher and your friends list, works the same at any age.

Deleting your data

In the app: Account & Privacy → Erase all my data. It removes every saved credential, your timetable, assignments, watchlist, friends data and all scheduled reminders, and signs you out of U of T.

Deleting the app removes its files, but keychain items can survive a reinstall, so use Erase first if that matters to you.

Two things live outside the app and must be removed separately:

  • The Quercus access token. It is registered on your U of T account. Remove it at Quercus → Account → Settings → Approved Integrations, where it appears as "Oakhall app".
  • Friends data in CloudKit. Removing your friends and signing out clears what the app wrote. Your schedule lives in your own iCloud account, so deleting the app's data in Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage removes it too. To have any remainder in the public database deleted, contact the address below.
